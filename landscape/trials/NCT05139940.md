## Summary

A prospective observational cohort study that measured how well a commercial computer-aided detection (CAD) algorithm reads digital chest X-rays for tuberculosis against a reference radiologist panel, run at three primary-care facilities in Lusaka, Zambia (Chainda South, Chawama, and Kanyama) [ct-001]. The study enrolled 2,432 participants across three populations relevant to case-finding: people with presumptive TB, household contacts of TB patients, and people newly diagnosed with HIV [ct-001]. It ran from November 2021 to November 2022 and is listed as completed [ct-001].

The design maps directly onto the question the WHO 2021 CAD recommendation left to programs: does the software hold up in the specific population and setting where it will be deployed? The primary outcome was the CAD algorithm's sensitivity and specificity against the radiologist panel, with an operating-point calibration during a pilot phase. The secondary outcome benchmarked performance against the WHO triage targets of 90% sensitivity and 70% specificity for TB detection, and 90% sensitivity with 50% specificity for a general abnormal-versus-normal read [ct-001]. The sponsor is the Centre for Infectious Disease Research in Zambia, a non-profit research institution rather than a software vendor [ct-001].

## Why it matters

This is the kind of independent, in-country validation the diagnostics theme treats as the strongest evidence a screening product can carry. A vendor's own accuracy figures are a starting point; a prospective study in the deployment population, with a locally calibrated operating point and an explicit test against the WHO target product profile, is what separates a marketing claim from a defensible one. The three-population enrollment (presumptive TB, contacts, people living with HIV) also probes the subgroups where CAD performance is known to shift, which is exactly where an implementer needs the numbers before committing.

## Watch for

- Publication of the primary results with per-population sensitivity and specificity, and whether the algorithm cleared the WHO 70% specificity bar at 90% sensitivity in this cohort.
- Whether performance held in the HIV-positive subgroup, where TB radiographic findings are frequently atypical and CAD tools have historically underperformed.
- Any follow-on implementation study that pairs the validated operating point with a confirmatory molecular test (for example Xpert MTB/RIF) to report the resulting reduction in molecular tests per case found.
